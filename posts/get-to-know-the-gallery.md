The Gallery element is a great way to showcase images on your Carrd site, but choosing the right style can make a big difference in how they look.

Carrd offers four Gallery styles: **Default, Fit, Fixed, and Full**. Each handles image sizing and layout a little differently. Here's how they work and when to use each one.

## Default

Each image keeps its original aspect ratio. The size you set acts as the **maximum height** for the images, so they scale up only as much as their original dimensions allow.

Use **Default** when you want images to preserve their proportions without being stretched or cropped.

## Fit

The size you set determines the **overall height** of the gallery. Images then expand to fill the gallery's width while maintaining their original proportions. Depending on the layout, some images may be cropped.

You can also fine-tune each image's **width and position individually**.

Use **Fit** when you want images to fill the gallery width dynamically across different screen sizes, and don't mind some cropping.

## Fixed

All images are given equal widths and fill the gallery. Since images may be cropped to fit, you can fine-tune their **position individually**, but not their width.

Use **Fixed** when you want all images to have a consistent size or prevent them from wrapping onto a new row as the screen gets narrower.

## Full

Each image fills the entire width of the gallery, regardless of its original aspect ratio. This means images may be stretched or distorted.

Use **Full** when you want each image to occupy its own row and don't need to preserve its original proportions.

This style can be especially convenient on mobile, where the available screen width is often smaller. Carrd also provides the **Full** option for all four styles when manual mobile settings are enabled.

---

Once you understand how these four styles handle image sizing, choosing the right one becomes much easier. Think about whether you want to preserve image proportions, fill the available space, or keep every image consistent.
